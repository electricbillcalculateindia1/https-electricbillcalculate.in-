📊 What Are Slabs in Electricity Billing?
Understanding how telescopic slab billing systems work — and why it matters for your bill

📐 The Telescopic Slab System Explained

Most Indian states (like Bihar, Maharashtra, UP, and others) use a telescopic / progressive slab billing system. This means the electricity rate you pay depends on how many total units you consume per month — but different rates apply to different chunks of your consumption, not the entire amount at one rate.

📋 Sample Domestic Tariff Slabs (Example: Bihar 2026)
0 – 125 units
₹0.00
FREE (subsidy)

💡 How It Works

Different rates for different chunks

If you use 250 units in Bihar, the first 125 units are completely free (₹0.00). Only the remaining 125 units (126–250) are billed at ₹5.52/unit = ₹690.00 in energy charges.

⚖️ Why Progressive?

Protects low-income households

Light users (below 100 units/month) pay the lowest rate. Higher consumption enters costlier slabs — encouraging energy efficiency across all income groups.

🔢 Why Is It Complex?

Manual calculation is confusing

You must calculate each slab separately, then add fixed charges, meter rent, electricity duty (5%), and FAC. Our calculator does all of this in one click.

🎯 The Slab Trap

One extra unit can change your slab

Using 126 units instead of 125 means the extra 1 unit gets billed at ₹5.52. Staying at or below 125 units keeps your energy charge at ₹0 — a huge saving for light users.

🧮 Worked Example — 250 Units (Domestic)

🟢 First 125 units × ₹0.00 (FREE) 125 × 0.00 = ₹0.00
🔴 Next 125 units (126–250) × ₹5.52 125 × 5.52 = ₹690.00
⚙️ Fixed Charge flat = ₹80.00

🏛️ Electricity Duty (5% of energy) 5% × ₹690.00 = ₹34.50

💡 Energy Saving Tips for Lower Bills
Practical ways to reduce your monthly electricity consumption — with real savings estimates
☀️

Go Solar — The Big Win

🌟 Best Investment

Most Indian regions receive 4–6 peak sun hours per day, making rooftop solar ideal for energy savings. A 1 kW system generates around 100–120 units/month, slashing your bill significantly. With net metering, you can even earn credits for surplus power fed back to the grid.

💰 Saves 60–80% on electricity bills
💡

Switch Every Bulb to LED

⚡ Quick Win

A single 60W incandescent bulb replaced by a 9W LED cuts that bulb's power use by 85%. If you replace 10 bulbs and run them 6 hours/day, you save around 15 units/month — that's ₹73–₹115 every month depending on your slab.

⚡ Saves ~15 units/month per 10 bulbs
🌬️

Fans Before AC — Always

🔥 High Impact

A ceiling fan uses just 50–75W while a 1.5-ton AC uses 1,200–1,500W — that's 20x more. In many Indian regions, fans alone are comfortable for much of the year. Using a fan for 4 hours instead of AC saves around 5–6 units per day, or 150+ units monthly.

🌬️ AC vs Fan = 20× power difference
🔋

Smart Inverter / Battery Usage

🧠 Smart Choice

Never charge heavy appliances (geysers, irons, induction cooktops) through your inverter/UPS. These draw massive current, wear out your battery faster, and increase charging cycles — meaning you use more grid electricity overall. Keep inverter loads light: fans, lights, phones only.

🔋 Extends battery life by 30–40%
🔌

Eliminate Phantom / Standby Power

👻 Hidden Drain

TVs, set-top boxes, Wi-Fi routers, microwave ovens, laptop chargers and gaming consoles consume 5–25W even when not in use — this is called "phantom load." Over a month, 5 devices in standby can waste 10–15 units. Use power strips with switches and turn them off at night.

👻 Phantom loads = 5–10% of your bill
🧊

Optimise Your Refrigerator

🧊 Always On

Your fridge runs 24/7 and accounts for 15–20% of home electricity use. Keep it at 37–40°F (3–4°C), never over-fill or leave it near heat sources. Clean the condenser coils every 6 months. A 5-star BEE-rated model uses 30–40% less power than an old 2-star one.

🧊 Fridge = 15–20% of home bill
🚿

Replace Geyser with Solar Water Heater

☀️ Solar Win

An electric geyser is one of the biggest energy hogs at home — a 2000W geyser running for just 1 hour uses 2 kWh. Over a month that's 60 units, costing ₹290–₹460. A solar water heater (₹15,000–₹25,000 one-time cost) pays back in 2–3 years with free hot water after.

🚿 Saves 50–60 units/month
📊

Track Your Monthly Units

🎯 Stay Slab-Smart

Use this calculator every month. Staying at or under 125 units keeps your energy charge at ₹0.00 — completely free. Above 125 units, every extra unit is billed at ₹5.52. Knowing where you stand can save real money.

📊 Slab awareness = smarter spending
🌀

Use 5-Star Rated Appliances Only

⭐ Long Term

BEE (Bureau of Energy Efficiency) star ratings matter a lot. A 5-star AC uses 30–40% less electricity than a 2-star equivalent. When replacing any major appliance — AC, washing machine, fridge — always choose the highest star rating available. The extra cost recovers in 1–2 years of savings.

⭐ 5-star saves 30–40% vs 2-star

How Electricity Billing Works in India

India's electricity distribution companies (DISCOMs) use a telescopic slab billing system approved by state electricity regulatory commissions such as BERC, UPERC, MSEDCL, and BESCOM. Under this system, lower per-unit rates apply to lower consumption slabs, and higher rates apply only to the units consumed within higher slabs — not the entire bill. This makes manual calculation complex, but our free bijli bill calculator handles it automatically for all major Indian states.

State-wise Electricity Tariff Rates 2026

Electricity tariff rates in India differ significantly across states. As of 2026: Bihar (NBPDCL/SBPDCL) domestic tariff — first 125 units free (₹0.00), above 125 units at ₹5.52/kWh. Uttar Pradesh (UPPCL) domestic tariff is ₹3.35 to ₹7.95/kWh. Delhi (BSES/Tata Power) is ₹3.00 to ₹8.00/kWh with subsidy applicable up to 200 units. Maharashtra (MSEDCL) has one of the highest tariff ranges at ₹3.44 to ₹11.82/kWh. Telangana (TSSPDCL/TSNPDCL) has a wide range of ₹1.45 to ₹9.95/kWh. Use the calculator above to see exact rates for your state.

Electricity Bill Components Explained

Your electricity bill in India typically includes: Energy Charges — the main cost based on units consumed × slab rate. Fixed Charges — a monthly charge based on your connection capacity (kVA/kW), regardless of consumption. Meter Rent — a small monthly fee for the electricity meter. Electricity Duty — a state government levy, typically 5–21% of energy charges. FAC (Fuel Adjustment Charges) — a quarterly variable surcharge based on fuel cost fluctuations. Our bijli bill calculator automatically applies all these components so you see the complete estimated electricity bill for any Indian state.
